RTTP/IQA -03: the declared lease boundary, the no-extend rule, and the two answer modes — nine records from the aicent-lease-boundary family

Following the records filed in #453 and the refusal-reason corpus built from them, the lease family that contributed one record to that filing is presented here as a full case set. The family is merged into the LFDT Agent-Authority-Conformance lab mainline (PR [#212](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/212), 3 November) and sealed with per-file SHA-256 at https://iqa.org/conformance/aicent-lease-boundary/ — fifteen cases: 9 conformant, 5 nonconformant, 1 ambiguous; author's runs 15/15 agree, 17/17 mutants killed, exit 0.

Texts: draft-li-rttp-iqa-addressing-03 (https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-li-rttp-iqa-addressing-03.txt, retrieved copy pinned at sha256 73470850daae844c51f281d6acf6d74419bb1b8c762270f1c411469c78cbc52c), sections 6.1, 6.2, 6.3 and the verification procedure of section 7.2. Canonical sources: https://github.com/Aicent-Stack/conformance — candidates/aicent-lease-boundary (https://github.com/Aicent-Stack/conformance/tree/main/candidates/aicent-lease-boundary; CANDIDATE.md, vectors.json, check.py, mutate.py), mirrored in the lab mainline at https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/tree/main/candidates/aicent-lease-boundary. The family is self-contained: every case states the facts at answer time, one response an implementation could produce, the expected verdict, and which rule decides.

One revision note before the records. The sealed lab records keep their revision-02 pins (sha256 02491c5a...); the cases retain proposed behavior and were re-assessed against revision 03, whose section numbering is unchanged. Revision 03 matters to this family in two places, both answering questions this repository's corpus discussion raised: section 6.1 now attaches an explicit consequence to the declared boundary ("A response that contradicts the deployment's declared boundary — a permit at an instant the declaration reads as expired, or an expiry verdict at an instant it reads as fresh — is nonconformant."), and section 7.2 now states that frame acceptance is not reliance ("Accepting a frame under this section means the bytes are well-formed and authenticate; it does not by itself constitute reliance or authorisation, which belong to the consumer's evaluation (Section 8) and to the caller's own policy."). The cases below already encode both positions; under -02 they were scored as readings, under -03 they are text-backed.

Mapping to the contract used here (offered as a crosswalk, not a claim of equivalence):

| this suite | our draft (-03) |
| --- | --- |
| time-bound evidence, the horizon rule at-or-past settled on the WIMSE list | sect. 6.1 makes the boundary a declared convention: "Whether the lease instant itself counts as fresh or as expired is a deployment convention; implementations SHOULD declare which boundary they use, and conformance material MAY test both." |
| refusing to extend stale evidence | sect. 6.1: "An answer whose lease has expired MUST be treated as expired; a verifier MUST NOT extend, renew, or reinterpret an expired lease except by obtaining a new answer." |
| evaluation-side policy vs. the wire answer | sect. 6.2 fixes two answer modes: live mode fails closed ("an organ that cannot be reached is not read as silent consent"), cached-lease mode splits by declared policy ("a policy that demands a current observation refuses the call with authority_not_determined (Section 8.1); a policy that accepts lease-fresh evidence may admit it, with any exposure bounded by the lease itself.") |
| defaults are not conformance targets | sect. 6.1: "Operators MAY configure other window lengths; the mechanism does not depend on this value." — the 120-second figure is a deployment observation, not a test value |

Records 1-4 — the declared boundary (R-DECLARED):

**Record 1 (LB-D01/D02, paired): both declared readings are conformant.** Facts: the read happens exactly at the lease instant; the deployment declared the inclusive convention (D01) or the exclusive convention (D02). Expected verdict: the permit under the declared inclusive boundary is conformant (D01); the expired refusal under the declared exclusive boundary is also conformant (D02) — sect. 6.1 licenses both and asks (SHOULD) for a declaration.

**Record 2 (LB-D03): a declaration that contradicts use.** Facts: the deployment declared the inclusive convention but answers expired at the boundary instant. Expected verdict: nonconformant. Under -02 this case rested on a strict reading, disclosed as such in the CANDIDATE; -03's new consequence sentence makes it text-backed.

**Record 3 (LB-D04): no declaration, unsettled by design.** Facts: the read happens at the boundary instant and no boundary is declared. Expected verdict: ambiguous, scored in named parts — the answer sits inside a licensed convention (conformant as to the answer) while the declaration part cannot be settled. Regression: a checker that forces either verdict on undeclared facts fails exactly this case's honesty, not its logic.

**Record 4 (LB-D05): frame acceptance does not decide reliance.** Facts: the envelope frame is accepted at the boundary instant — the verification procedure of sect. 7.2 rejects only when "lease_expiry is earlier than the current time" — and the answer is then refused under the declared exclusive convention. Expected verdict: conformant. Frame validity and reliance are different layers; the wire floor does not decide the convention. This is the one case where the reading was disputed before the text settled it: an external blind reading of this family against -02 (public lab record, attributed there) agreed with these expected verdicts on 14 of 15 cases and marked exactly this one ambiguous. The question it raised — whether accepting the frame at the instant makes the lease fresh at the answer layer — is now answered in the text: revision 03's section 7.2 states that acceptance "does not by itself constitute reliance or authorisation", which is what this case scores.

Records 5-8 — expiry and the two modes:

**Record 5 (LB-E01/E02, paired): no replaying an expired lease as fresh.** Facts: the answer is read past its lease (expiry 1100000 ms, read at 1200000 ms). Expected verdict: answering evidence_expired is conformant (E01); answering the same facts as a permit carrying the original observation as if current is nonconformant (E02) — "An answer read past its expiry MUST be answered as expired (Section 8.1), never replayed as fresh" (sect. 6.3). A locally added grace period fails the same way (LB-E03).

**Record 6 (LB-M01/M02, paired): live mode fails closed regardless of the cache.** Facts: live mode, organ unreachable, a cached answer within its lease exists. Expected verdict: refusal with authority_not_determined is conformant (M01, observation member null — the answer relied on no observation); admitting the fresh cache as a permit is nonconformant (M02) — "Every call in this mode is expected to be refused when the organ is unreachable, regardless of the age of any cached answer."

**Record 7 (LB-M03/M04, paired): cached-lease mode splits by declared policy.** Facts: cached-lease mode, cache within its lease, organ unreachable, declared policy demanding a current observation (M03) or accepting lease-fresh evidence (M04). Expected verdict: both are conformant — the policy demanding currency refuses with authority_not_determined; the policy accepting lease-fresh evidence may admit, "with any exposure bounded by the lease itself." The mode is distinguishable by more than a flag: under the same revocation timeline the two modes yield different answers.

**Record 8 (LB-M05): the expired cache wins the priority order.** Facts: cached-lease mode, the cache itself past its lease, organ unreachable. Expected verdict: conformant — refused as evidence_expired, the first applicable value in the sect. 8.1 priority; the unreachable organ adds nothing to an answer whose lease has already expired. This record ties the lease family to the refusal-reason corpus already filed here.

Record 9 — the window (R-WINDOW):

**Record 9 (LB-W01/W02, paired): the stated expiry is the check, not the default.** Facts: an answer whose stated lease (300 seconds, operator-configured) has not expired. Expected verdict: the permit is conformant (W01); refusing it by assuming the 120-second deployment default instead of checking the stated expiry is nonconformant (W02).

What these records do not claim: the author's runs say nothing about any implementation, and they do not show that the expected results are the right reading of the source — the reading is written out in the CANDIDATE for anyone to dispute, sentence by sentence, and a correction in a later draft revision overrides the checker. No implementation has been run against these cases beyond the author's own checker-and-mutant batteries.
